01 / The operating loop

A repeatable site walk.
A traceable project update.

A quadruped robot carries the capture equipment. BIM supplies the building context. The schedule supplies the planned sequence. Together, they support a reviewed record of what has changed.

01 — CAPTURE

Walk the site

Follow an approved route and capture dated video or images. Add 360° cameras or laser scanning when the task requires them.

02 — LOCATE

Link to BIM

Associate each observation with a level, room, zone, or element. Use manual mapping first; validate pose and model alignment for automation.

03 — COMPARE

Check the plan

Compare usable evidence against model elements, previous visits, and schedule activities due by the inspection date.

04 — VERIFY

Review the evidence

AI suggests visible states and changes. A reviewer confirms the stage reached, corrects mismatches, or requests another view.

05 — ACT

Update the team

Publish reviewed progress, assign location-linked issues, and plan follow-up captures. Keep evidence attached to every update.

The key distinction: a robot visiting an area proves capture coverage. Progress requires usable evidence of the relevant work and an agreed completion rule.

02 / Three connected inputs

What happened.
Where. Against which plan.

BIM means Building Information Modeling: geometry connected to information about spaces and elements. Linking those elements to construction activities enables planned-versus-observed comparison.

A / ROBOT CAPTURE

The observed condition

  • Timestamped video, stills, or 360° imagery.
  • Robot pose and camera calibration, when available.
  • Optional registered point clouds for geometric checks.
  • Repeat visits to show changes over time.
The sensor payload determines what can be assessed. RGB video does not provide thermal readings or survey-grade dimensions.
B / BIM MODEL

The project context

  • Levels, rooms, work areas, and element geometry.
  • Stable element identifiers and model version.
  • Element type, trade, and expected quantities.
  • Coordinates aligned with the capture data.
Model geometry alone does not establish when work is due or whether it has passed inspection.
C / CONSTRUCTION PLAN

The expected progress

  • Activities, work packages, and baseline dates.
  • Links from activities to model elements.
  • Measurement units and agreed stage criteria.
  • Responsible teams and inspection requirements.
Define whether “complete” means installed, inspected, tested, or accepted before calculating progress.

These values are invented to explain the workflow; they are not measured from the supplied factory videos. A gap in verified installation is a review signal, not proof of a schedule delay. “pp” means percentage points.

Use a defined denominator

Verified installation = quantity confirmed at the agreed stage ÷ total package quantity. Measure each package consistently by count, length, area, or an agreed weight. Do not average unlike packages without defined weights.

Keep uncertainty visible

Unknown elements remain in the package total and are reported separately. Work hidden by walls or poor camera views needs another source of evidence. Visible installation does not establish testing or acceptance.

Current evidence: 10 videos · approximately 20 minutes · 1080p. These recordings show an operating factory. They demonstrate capture and inspection workflows; construction progress figures and model locations in this concept are illustrative. The agreed demo uses a representative model because no actual project BIM has been supplied.
Can the system automatically determine that work is complete?

Automation can propose observable states when the views and model alignment are sufficient. Each trade needs explicit stage rules and validation. Hidden work, testing, quality acceptance, and contractual completion may require inspection records or other evidence. Start with reviewer approval before publishing progress.

Can ordinary video produce an accurate as-built BIM?

Video can support visual documentation and, with suitable capture and calibration, reconstruction experiments. A measured as-built workflow needs validated geometry, scale, registration, and adequate coverage. Laser scans or surveyed control may be needed for the required tolerance.

What is needed to turn this demo into a pilot?

A current project model and coordinate reference, a schedule mapped to the chosen work package, agreed quantity and completion rules, repeat captures along a suitable route, and a site reviewer who can validate results. Establish baseline performance on that package before increasing scope.
